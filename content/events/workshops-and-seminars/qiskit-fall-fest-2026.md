## About

Qiskit Fall Fest is the annual series of student-run quantum computing events that IBM Quantum supports on campuses around the world every fall. Clemson Quantum Club brings the Fall Fest back to Clemson in 2026. This year’s theme marks ten years since IBM put the first quantum computer on the cloud, and a decade of open access that lets anyone run programs on real quantum hardware.

Our Fall Fest runs all week. Every day opens with a seminar on that day’s topic, led by a guest speaker from research or industry on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday and by the organizers on Tuesday and Thursday. Every seminar is followed by a guided Qiskit notebook that you run on both a simulator and a real IBM quantum computer. The week runs from your first circuit to experiments drawn from the past year of quantum computing papers. Every session starts from the basics, so you do not need any quantum background. If you want a head start, the [Learning Resources](/resources/learning-resources/) page has beginner tutorials you can work through before the week.

## Week at a Glance

Every day pairs a seminar with a notebook lab. Every lab uses a simulator for the ideal answer and sends at least one job to real IBM hardware for the measured one. Come for one day or all five; each day stands on its own. Open a day to see the plan; the notebook, slides, notes, and seminar links will be posted there as they are ready. <a href="https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Create a free IBM Quantum account</a> before you arrive so your first job runs on Monday, November 16.

We put it to work on famous experiments, the double slit as a one-qubit circuit, Schrödinger’s cat, and the CHSH game, where Bell correlations rule out local hidden variables. We also look back to 2016, when IBM put the first quantum processor on the cloud, and at what has changed in the ten years since.</p>

Then the ladder from error suppression to mitigation, detection, and correction, including quantum teleportation with mid-circuit measurement and feed-forward, the trick behind long-range entanglement on today’s chips. Everything runs on both a simulator and real hardware, so you can compare ideal statistics with what a noisy processor returns.</p>

<p class="qff-eyebrow">04 / FAQ</p>

## FAQ

<div class="qff-faq">
  <details class="qff-faq-item">
    <summary>Do I need quantum computing or Qiskit experience?</summary>
    <p>No. The seminars assume no background, and the labs start with your very first circuit.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="qff-faq-item">
    <summary>Do I have to attend the whole week?</summary>
    <p>No. Every day stands on its own, so come for one day or the whole week.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="qff-faq-item">
    <summary>What should I bring?</summary>
    <p>A laptop every day. <a href="https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Create a free IBM Quantum account</a> (Open Plan) before Monday, November 16; we will help you install Qiskit and connect your account in the first session.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="qff-faq-item">
    <summary>Do the labs really run on quantum hardware?</summary>
    <p>Yes. Each lab sends at least one small job to an IBM quantum processor through your own account, alongside a simulator run for comparison. The free plan gives each person about ten minutes of processor time every 28 days, and the whole week uses well under that.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="qff-faq-item">
    <summary>Can I run the notebooks without installing anything?</summary>
    <p>Yes. Each notebook will open in Google Colab from the link on this page once it is posted.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="qff-faq-item">
    <summary>Is it free?</summary>
    <p>Yes. Qiskit Fall Fest at Clemson is free to attend. Registration is only so we know how many people to expect.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="qff-faq-item">
    <summary>Who can attend?</summary>
    <p>Anyone curious about quantum computing. Clemson undergraduate and graduate students from any major are the main audience, and you do not need to be a member of Clemson Quantum Club.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="qff-faq-item">
    <summary>Is it in person or online?</summary>
    <p>In person at Clemson University, November 16–20. Rooms will be posted here once they are confirmed, and everyone who registers gets an email. The seminars are also livestreamed, and the recordings are posted on this page afterward along with the notebooks, slides, and notes.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="qff-faq-item">
    <summary>Are there prizes or certificates?</summary>
    <p>No. This is a week of learning, not a competition, so there is no judging and nothing to win. What you leave with is a set of notebooks you have run on real quantum hardware.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="qff-faq-item">
    <summary>How do I register?</summary>
    <p>Use the <a href="https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSeQCj_aRX5uNkn9QCCYyE3kMUOUVrye1IkdurmFdaTUMwwRBw/viewform" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">registration form</a>, also linked at the top of this page. Registration is free.</p>
  </details>
